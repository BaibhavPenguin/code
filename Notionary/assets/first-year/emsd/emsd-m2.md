## <u>Overview of Natural Resources</U>
---
### Definition of Resource
A **resource** is anything that has utility, value, and can be used to satisfy human needs or achieve a specific purpose. Resources may be natural, human-made, or human resources.
* **Meaning:** A material becomes a resource only when humans recognize its usefulness and have the technology and knowledge to utilize it. *(Example: Crude oil had little value before the invention of petroleum-refining technology.)*

### Definition of Natural Resources
**Natural resources** are materials and substances that occur naturally in the environment and can be used by humans to meet their needs without being created by human beings.
* **Examples:** Air, water, soil, sunlight, forests, wildlife, minerals, coal, petroleum, natural gas.

### Importance of Natural Resources
* Provide food, water, and shelter.
* Supply raw materials for industries.
* Generate energy for homes, transport, and industries.
* Support agriculture and economic growth.
* Maintain ecological balance and biodiversity.
* Create employment and improve living standards.
* **Examples:** Coal is used to generate electricity; petroleum is refined into petrol, diesel, and aviation fuel; forests provide timber, medicines, oxygen, and wildlife habitats.



## <u>Classification of Natural Resources</u>
---
### A. Based on Origin
* **Biotic Resources:** Derived from living organisms or living components of nature.
  * *Examples:* Forests, animals, fish, crops, microorganisms.
* **Abiotic Resources:** Derived from non-living components of nature.
  * *Examples:* Air, water, soil, minerals, sunlight.

### B. Based on Renewability
* **Renewable Resources:** Can regenerate naturally within a short time frame.
  * *Examples:* Solar energy, wind energy, water, forests, biomass.
* **Non-Renewable Resources:** Finite resources that take millions of years to form and cannot be easily replenished.
  * *Examples:* Coal, petroleum, natural gas, iron ore, gold.

### C. Based on Physical State
* **Solid Resources:** Coal, iron ore, copper, limestone, sand.
* **Liquid Resources:** Water, petroleum, groundwater, crude oil, mineral oil.
* **Gaseous Resources:** Natural gas, oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide, helium.



## <u>Forest Resources</u>
---

### Introduction
Forest resources are all the goods and services obtained from forests that support human life, economic development, and environmental sustainability. They include timber, fuelwood, medicinal plants, wildlife, and ecological services such as climate regulation and soil conservation.

### Consequences of Forest Degradation
1. **Loss of Biodiversity:** Destruction of habitats leads to the decline and extinction of plant and animal species (e.g., Royal Bengal Tiger, Asian Elephant).
2. **Climate Change:** Fewer trees absorb less carbon dioxide, increasing greenhouse gases and contributing to global warming.
3. **Soil Erosion:** Deforestation exposes topsoil to wind and rain, causing fertility loss and erosion.
4. **Water Scarcity:** Decreased forest cover reduces groundwater recharge, causing rivers and streams to dry up.
5. **Floods and Landslides:** Removal of forest roots that absorb rainwater and stabilize slopes increases flood and landslide risks.
6. **Loss of Livelihoods:** Rural and tribal communities lose primary sources of food, fuelwood, medicines, and income.

### Major Challenges in Forest Management
1. **Deforestation:** Large-scale clearing for agriculture, urbanization, and infrastructure.
2. **Illegal Logging:** Unauthorized cutting and smuggling of timber (e.g., Sandalwood and Teak).
3. **Forest Fires:** Seasonal fires caused by natural events or human negligence (e.g., Uttarakhand forest fires).
4. **Encroachment & Urbanization:** Expansion of human settlements into forest lands.
5. **Human–Wildlife Conflict:** Shrinking habitats force wild animals into agricultural fields and villages (e.g., Elephants in Assam and Karnataka).
6. **Climate Change:** Changing rainfall patterns and rising temperatures cause droughts and pest attacks.
7. **Overexploitation:** Excessive collection of fuelwood, timber, and non-timber forest products.
8. **Invasive Species:** Non-native species outcompeting native flora (e.g., *Lantana camara*).
9. **Lack of Community Participation:** Insufficient involvement of local indigenous communities.
10. **Weak Enforcement:** Limited staff, funding, and modern monitoring technology.



## <u>Grasslands</u>
---

### Definition & Characteristics
A **grassland** is a terrestrial ecosystem dominated by grasses and herbaceous plants, with sparse trees and shrubs, receiving moderate rainfall (25–75 cm annually).
* **Characteristics:** Highly fertile soils, adapted to periodic droughts and natural fires, found in tropical, temperate, and alpine regions.

### Major Challenges in Grassland Management
1. **Overgrazing:** Excessive livestock grazing reduces vegetative cover and causes soil degradation (e.g., Banni Grasslands in Gujarat).
2. **Conversion to Agriculture:** Conversion of fertile grasslands into agricultural land.
3. **Urbanization & Fragmentation:** Infrastructure expansion destroying continuous grassland habitats.
4. **Invasive Species:** Colonization by aggressive non-native species (e.g., *Prosopis juliflora*).
5. **Climate Change:** Irregular rainfall accelerating land degradation and desertification.
6. **Forest Plantations:** Misguided tree-planting drives in natural grasslands that alter open-habitat dynamics.
7. **Threat to Wildlife:** Decline of specialized species like the Great Indian Bustard, Blackbuck, and Indian Wolf.
8. **Neglect:** Misclassification of natural grasslands as "wastelands."


## <u>Wetlands</u>
---

### Overview
Wetlands are land areas covered permanently or seasonally with shallow water, forming a transition zone between terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems.
* **Inland Wetlands:** Lakes, rivers, marshes, swamps, floodplains.
* **Coastal Wetlands:** Mangroves, estuaries, coastal salt marshes.

### Major Functions of Wetlands
1. **Flood Control:** Act as natural sponges to absorb excess stormwater.
2. **Water Purification:** Filter sediments, nutrients, and toxic pollutants.
3. **Groundwater Recharge:** Allow surface water to percolate into underground aquifers.
4. **Habitat for Biodiversity:** Provide breeding and nesting grounds for aquatic life and migratory birds (e.g., Chilika Lake).
5. **Carbon Sequestration:** Store large quantities of carbon, aiding climate regulation.
6. **Soil Protection:** Vegetation buffers shorelines against erosion.
7. **Socio-Economic Support:** Support commercial fisheries, agriculture, and ecotourism.

### Challenges Faced by Wetlands
* **Pollution:** Untreated industrial waste, urban sewage, and agricultural run-off.
* **Encroachment & Land Reclamation:** Filling of wetlands for real estate and infrastructure.
* **Water Over-extraction:** Upstream diversion drying up wetland flow.
* **Invasive Species:** Proliferation of fast-growing weeds like Water Hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*).
* **Poaching & Illegal Fishing:** Over-harvesting disrupting ecological balance.



## <u> Wildlife and Aquatic Resources </u>
---
### Wildlife Resources
Wild plants and animals living in natural habitats without human intervention. They maintain food webs, preserve genetic diversity, and support ecotourism.

### Aquatic Resources
Organisms inhabiting marine and freshwater environments (fish, crustaceans, corals, seaweed). They support international trade, human nutrition, and global oxygen production.

> **Key Fact:** India occupies 2.4% of global land area but hosts 7–8% of recorded species, making it one of the world's 17 megadiverse countries. It contains 4 global biodiversity hotspots: **Himalaya**, **Western Ghats**, **Indo-Burma**, and **Sundaland**.

### Primary Causes of Biodiversity Loss
1. Habitat loss, fragmentation, and deforestation.
2. Environmental pollution (chemical, plastic, thermal).
3. Climate change and extreme weather.
4. Overexploitation and illegal wildlife trade/poaching.
5. Invasion of non-native alien species.



## <u>Microbes as a Resource</u>
---

Microscopic organisms (bacteria, fungi, microalgae, protozoa) serve as important biological resources:

* **Agriculture:** Biofertilizers (*Rhizobium*, *Azotobacter*) fix nitrogen and enhance soil fertility.
* **Food Processing:** *Lactobacillus* for dairy fermentation (curd, yogurt); *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (yeast) for baking.
* **Pharmaceuticals:** Antibiotic production (e.g., Penicillin from *Penicillium*, Streptomycin from *Streptomyces*).
* **Waste Management & Bioenergy:** Anaerobic digestors using methanogenic bacteria to process waste and produce biogas (methane); bioremediation of polluted soils.

### Sustainable Water Management Practices
* **Rainwater Harvesting:** Rooftop collection systems (e.g., mandatory implementation in Chennai).
* **Micro-Irrigation:** Drip and sprinkler systems to improve water-use efficiency in agriculture.
* **Effluent Treatment & Recycling:** Industrial wastewater reclamation for non-potable uses.
* **Government Initiatives:** Comprehensive conservation plans such as the *Namami Gange Programme*.

### Environmental Impacts of Over-exploitation & Water Scarcity
* **Over-exploitation Impacts:** Depletion of water tables, drying of surface ecosystems, land subsidence, soil salinization, and coastal saltwater intrusion.

## <u>Sustainable Development</u>
---

### Definition
Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs *(Brundtland Commission, 1987)*.

### Three Pillars of Sustainability
1. **Environmental Sustainability:** Conservation of biodiversity, protection of natural ecosystems, and reduction of emissions.
2. **Economic Sustainability:** Generating long-term economic growth without exhausting natural capital.
3. **Social Sustainability:** Universal access to basic human needs, poverty reduction, gender equity, and social justice.
