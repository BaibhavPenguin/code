# Biodiversity &mdash; Module 1  
**Biodiversity** is the variety of life on Earth. It includes all living organisms, the genes they contain, and the ecosystems in which they live.

## <u>Importance of Biodiversity</u>
---
- Maintains ecological balance.
- Provides food, medicine, and raw materials.
- Supports pollination, soil fertility, and clean water.
- Helps ecosystems adapt to environmental changes.
- Contributes to economic activities like agriculture, fisheries, and tourism.

## <u>Levels of Biodiversity</u>
---

Biological Diversity is divided into three levels :

### <u>1 &mdash; Genetic Diversity</u>
Genetic diversity means variation in genes within the same species.Genes determine the characteristics of living organisms such as colour, size, height and disease resistance.  

**Examples :** Different varieties of rice, Different breeds of dogs, etc.  

### <u>2 &mdash; Species Diversity</u>
Species diversity means the variety of different species living in an area.  
**Examples :** A forest contains Tigers, Deer, Monkeys, Birds, Insects, Snakes, etc.  

### <u>3 &mdash; Ecosystem Diversity</u>  
Ecosystem diversity means the variety of different ecosystems present in a region.  
**Examples :** India has different ecosystems like Forests, Grasslands, Deserts, Wetlands, Mountains, Aquatic (Lakes, Rivers, Oceans).  

## <u>Biogeographic Zones of India</u>
---
A biogeographic zone is a large natural region with similar climate, landforms, plants and animals. India is one of the world's 17 mega-diverse countries because it has a wide variety of ecosystems and species.  
India has **10 Major Biogeographic Zones.**  

### Trans-Himalayan Region.  
**Location:** Ladakh and parts of Jammu & Kashmir.   
**Features:** Cold desert, Very low rainfall, High mountains.  
**Animals:** Snow leopard, Yak, Tibetan wild ass.  

### Himalayan Region.  
**Location:** Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh. 
**Features:** Snow-covered mountains, Dense forests, Rich biodiversity.    
**Animals:** Red panda, Himalayan bear and Musk deer.


### Indian Desert  
**Location:** Rajasthan and Gujarat  
**Features:** Very hot climate, low rainfall, sandy soil  
**Animals:** Camel, desert fox, Great Indian Bustard  

### Semi-Arid Zone  
**Location:** Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat, and parts of Maharashtra  
**Features:** Grasslands, shrubs, moderate rainfall  
**Animals:** Blackbuck, wolf, Nilgai  

### Western Ghats  
**Location:** Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu  
**Features:** Dense forests, heavy rainfall, biodiversity hotspot  
**Animals:** Lion-tailed macaque, Nilgiri Tahr, Malabar giant squirrel  

### Deccan Peninsula  
**Location:** Central and Southern India  
**Features:** Plateaus, deciduous forests, rich wildlife  
**Animals:** Tiger, leopard, elephant  

### Gangetic Plain  
**Location:** Northern India  
**Features:** Fertile soil, rivers, agriculture  
**Animals:** Ganges River dolphin, crocodile, freshwater fish  

### North-East India  
**Location:** Assam, Meghalaya, Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh, and Sikkim   
**Features:** Heavy rainfall, Dense forests, Very high biodiversity   
**Animals:** One-horned rhinoceros, Hoolock gibbon, clouded leopard   

### Islands  
**Location:** Andaman & Nicobar Islands and Lakshadweep  
**Features:** Coral reefs, mangroves, tropical forests  
**Animals:** Sea turtle, dugong, saltwater crocodile  

### Coastal Zone  
**Location:** Eastern and Western coast of India  
**Features:** Beaches, mangroves, estuaries  
**Animals:** Dolphins, crabs, Olive Ridley turtle  

## <u>Altitudinal Gradient</u>
---
As altitude (height above sea level) increases, biodiversity generally decreases.  
**Example:** Lower Himalayan forests have many species while Snow-covered mountain peaks have fewer species.  

## <u>Biodiversity Hot-spots</u>
---
A biodiversity hot-spot is a region with very high biodiversity and many endemic species, but it is also under serious threat due to habitat loss.  

### Biodiversity Hot-spots in India.

* Western Ghats-Sri Lanka
* Himalaya
* Indo-Burma
* Sundaland (includes Nicobar Islands)

## <u>India as a Mega-Diversity Nation</u>
---
Introduction: India is one of the 17 mega-biodiversity countries in the world. Although India occupies only about 2.4% of the Earth's land area, it is home to nearly 8% of the world's recorded species.This rich variety of plants, animals and ecosystems makes India a mega-biodiversity nation.  

**Definition:** A mega-biodiversity nation is a country that has an exceptionally large number of plant and animal species, many of which are found nowhere else in the world.  

## Why is India called a Mega-Diversity Nation
---
### 1 &mdash; Ecological Value  
**Definition:** Refers to the role of biodiversity in maintaining ecological balance and supporting natural processes.  
**Importance:** Maintains food chains/webs, nutrient cycling, soil fertility, climate regulation, crop pollination, pest control, and air/water quality  
**Examples:** Bees pollinate flowering plants; forests absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen; wetlands filter pollutants from water  

---

### 2 &mdash; Economic Value  
**Definition:** Refers to the direct and indirect financial benefits obtained from biodiversity.  
**Direct Uses:** Timber, medicines, fruits, fish, fuelwood, fibres  
**Indirect Uses:** Tourism, pollination, flood control, climate regulation  
**Examples:** Neem used in medicines; bamboo used in construction; national parks generating ecotourism income  
**Importance:** Supports agriculture, provides employment, contributes to national economy, reduces disaster management costs  

---

### 3 &mdash; Social Value  
**Definition:** Refers to the importance of biodiversity in supporting human society, culture, and livelihoods.  
**Importance:** Provides food/shelter, supports tribal and rural communities, preserves traditional knowledge, strengthens cultural identity, improves quality of life  
**Examples:** Sacred groves protected by local communities; indigenous peoples depending on forests for livelihood; traditional medicinal plants used in Ayurveda  

---

### 4 &mdash; Ethical Value  
**Definition:** The moral responsibility to protect all living organisms because every species has the right to exist.  
**Principles:** Respect for all life forms, conservation for future generations, preventing species extinction, sustainable resource use  
**Examples:** Wildlife conservation, protection of endangered species, ban on illegal wildlife trade  
**Importance:** Encourages environmental responsibility, promotes intergenerational equity, supports sustainable development  

---

### 5 &mdash; Aesthetic Value  
**Definition:** Refers to the beauty and recreational importance of nature.  
**Importance & Benefits:** Reduces stress, improves mental health, inspires art/literature/photography, encourages recreation and ecotourism  
**Examples:** Mountains, forests, waterfalls, coral reefs, national parks  

---

### 6 &mdash; Informational Value  
**Definition:** Refers to the knowledge, education, and scientific information obtained from biodiversity.  
**Importance & Benefits:** Scientific research and advancement, discovery of new medicines, agricultural improvement, genetic studies, environmental education  
**Examples:** Penicillin discovered from fungi; medicinal plants studied for new drugs; wildlife research improving conservation strategies.
