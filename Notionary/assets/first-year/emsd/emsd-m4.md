## <u>Meaning of Environmental Issues & Concept of Scale</u>
---

**Environmental issues** are problems or unwanted changes in the natural environment that adversely affect humans, flora, fauna, and entire ecosystems. Examples include air pollution, water pollution, deforestation, climate change, soil erosion, noise pollution, loss of biodiversity, and waste generation.

### What is "Scale" in Environmental Studies?
**Scale** refers to the geographic extent of an environmental problem, as well as the area and timeframe over which it operates. Environmental problems operate across two primary dimensions:

* **Spatial Scale:** The geographic area affected by an environmental phenomenon (e.g., a single street, an entire city, a state/country, or the whole planet).
* **Temporal Scale:** The duration over which an environmental phenomenon lasts (e.g., short-term hours/days, medium-term years, or long-term decades/centuries).

### Waste Management Concepts
* **Municipal Solid Waste (MSW):** Everyday garbage generated by households, offices, and commercial establishments (food scraps, paper, plastic, packaging).

## <u>Transboundary Air Pollution, Acid Rain, and Smog</u>
---

### Transboundary Air Pollution
Occurs when air pollutants originating in one country or state travel across political borders via atmospheric transport, damaging ecosystems in downwind regions.

### Acid Rain
Rainfall or precipitation made abnormally acidic by reactions between atmospheric moisture and emitted gases.

- Emissions (SO2 & NOx)
- Reaction with Atmospheric Water Vapour
- Formation of Sulfuric (H2SO4) & Nitric (HNO3) Acids
- Acid Precipitation (Rain, Snow, Fog)
- Damage to Soil, Aquatic Systems, Crops & Buildings


### Smog Types

* **Classical Smog:** Associated with heavy coal combustion in cool, humid conditions.
* **Photochemical Smog:** Triggered when vehicle exhaust gases (NOx and VOCs) react under intense sunlight to form ground-level ozone (O₃) and haze.

### Major Drivers of Current Biodiversity Loss
1. **Habitat Loss & Fragmentation:** Clearing natural land for infrastructure and agriculture.
2. **Deforestation:** Direct removal of critical forest habitats.
3. **Pollution:** Contamination of aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems with chemicals and plastics.
4. **Climate Change:** Shifting thermal belts, ocean warming, and coral bleaching.
5. **Overexploitation:** Overfishing, poaching, and unmanaged wildlife trade.
6. **Invasive Alien Species:** Non-native species outcompeting native fauna and flora (e.g., Water Hyacinth).

## <u>Ozone Layer Depletion & Global Climate Change</u>
---

### Ozone Layer Depletion
The stratospheric ozone O<sub>3</sub>layer shields Earth by absorbing harmful solar ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation.

- Release of Synthetic CFCs / Halons
- Transport to Stratosphere
- UV Radiation Breaks Down CFCs, Releasing Chlorin
- Chlorine Catalytically Destroys Ozone (O3) Molecules
- Increased UV-B Radiation Penetrates to Earth's Surface


* **Consequences:** Higher human rates of skin cancer and cataracts, damage to terrestrial plants, reduced phytoplankton productivity.
* **Control Mechanism:** The **Montreal Protocol** successfully phases out global production of ozone-depleting substances.

---

### Global Climate Change

#### The Enhanced Greenhouse Effect
Greenhouse gases (GHGs) like CO<sub>2</sub>, CH<sub>4</sub>, and N<sub>2</sub>O form an atmospheric blanket that traps infrared thermal heat. Human activities intensify this blanket, causing global warming.

- Fossil Fuel Combustion / Deforestation / Industrial Farming
- Surge in Atmospheric GHGs (CO<sub>3</sub>, CH<sub>4</sub>, N<sub>2</sub>O)
- Increased Retained Thermal Radiation (Enhanced Greenhouse Effect)
- Global Warming (Rising Surface Temperatures)
- Widespread Global Climate Change


#### Global Warming vs. Climate Change
* **Global Warming:** The long-term rise in the Earth's average surface temperature.
* **Climate Change:** Broad environmental shifts caused by global warming, including sea-level rise, melting ice caps, changed monsoons, and extreme weather events.

#### Response Strategies
* **Mitigation (Reducing Causes):** Transitioning to renewable energy, enhancing energy efficiency, protecting forest carbon sinks.
* **Adaptation (Managing Impacts):** Building coastal defenses, cultivating drought-resistant crop varieties, developing urban heat action plans.
