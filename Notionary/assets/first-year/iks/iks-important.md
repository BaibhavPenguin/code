### <u>1 &mdash; What do you mean by IKS(Indian Knowledge System)?</u>

IKS stands for Indian Knowledge Systems, which is the traditional wisdom, science, philosophy, and arts that developed in India over thousands of years.   
IKS includes a wide range of disciplines, philosophies, science & arts,
cultural practices that have been developed over a millennium. The Indian Knowledge System is preserved through traditional written text and practical appreciations.

**<u>Features of IKS</u>**  

**Holistic Approach to life:** The Indian knowledge system follows a holistic approach which means it focuses on the complete development of a person.  
**Philosophical Foundations :** Indian philosophy is one of the oldest and richest in the world. It explains the meaning of life, truth and reality through different schools of thought.  
**Spiritual Wisdom:** The Indian knowledge system has a rich collection of ancient scriptures and philosophical texts.  
**Scientific Contribution:** Ancient India made great contributions in Science and Technology and various other fields like Mathematics, Astronomy, Metallurgy, and Medicine.  

The Indian Knowledge System also consists of a rich cultural history with artistic expression such as Dance, Music and Literature. It also features practices which improve mental health and overall fitness through Yoga and Meditation.

### <u>2 &mdash; What do you mean by Macaulay's Education Policy?</u>

Macaulay's Education Policy, officially known as the <u>Minute on Indian Education</u>, was introduced in 1835 by Thomas Babington Macaulay.

#### Features of MEP
-  All public funds were restricted exclusively to Western literature and science taught through the English language.
-  It aimed to train a group of individuals who were "Indian in blood and colour, but English in tastes, opinions, morals, and intellect" to serve the British administration.
-  It famously dismissed traditional Indian and Arabic literature as inferior to European knowledge.
- Grants and printing support for traditional Oriental colleges and texts were stopped.

### <u>3 &mdash; Discuss the term Yoga and Well-Being?</u>

Ancient India had a profound tradition of yoga that integrated physical, mental, and spiritual practices aimed at promoting wellbeing and self-realization. The following are some key aspects of yoga and its relationship with wellbeing in ancient India

#### Origins & Philosophy
The practice of yoga dates back thousands of years, with its roots found in ancient texts such as the Vedas, Upanishads, and the Bhagavad Gita. Yoga aims for spiritual liberation (moksha) through various paths (yogas).

#### Physical Aspects **(Asanas)**
Ancient Indian texts like the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali and Hatha Yoga Pradipika describe various yoga postures (asanas) aimed at enhancing physical health and vitality.

#### Breathing Techniques **(Pranayama)**
Pranayama, or breath control techniques, were integral to yoga practice. They focused on regulating the breath to improve oxygenation, energy flow (prana), and mental clarity. 

#### Mental & Emotional Well Being **(Dharana & Dhyana)**
Yoga in ancient India emphasized mental discipline and emotional balance through practices such as concentration (dharana) and meditation (dhyana).

#### Philosophical Foundations   
The philosophical underpinnings of yoga, particularly as expounded in texts like the Yoga Sutras, emphasized ethical principles (yamas and niyamas) such as non-violence (ahimsa), truthfulness (satya), and self-discipline (tapas). These principles guided practitioners towards a balanced and harmonious life.

#### Integration with Ayurveda
Yoga and Ayurveda, the ancient Indian system of medicine, were often practiced together to promote holistic wellbeing. Ayurveda provided dietary guidelines, herbal remedies, and lifestyle practices that complemented yoga practices to maintain physical health and balance doshas (body energies).

#### Social and Cultural Context
Yoga was practiced in various settings, including monasteries, schools of philosophy (darshanas), and households. It was transmitted orally and through guru-disciple traditions, fostering community and social cohesion.

#### Spiritual Evolution
Ultimately, yoga in ancient India was viewed as a path to spiritual evolution and self-realization (atma-jnana). By integrating physical practices, breath control, ethical principles, and meditation, practitioners aimed to transcend worldly attachments and achieve inner peace and fulfillment.

### <u>4 &mdash; What do you understand by the term medicine?</u>
Medicine is the science and practice of diagnosing, treating and preventing diseases and restoring human health. It not only is a treatment to a disease but is also a method to prevent any future diseases.  
Since Ancient times, Ayurveda, the ancient medicine system paired with Yoga was used for prevention and treatment of diseases in India.
However, like many traditional systems, Ayurveda has not undergone as much modern clinical research as conventional Western medicine. Even so, many Ayurvedic practices have demonstrated clear benefits for health and well-being.  
**Some observed health benefits of Ayurveda include:**
- Relieves symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis.  
- Lowers blood pressure and cholesterol levels.  
- Supports heart health and lowers the risk of heart disease through dietary guidelines.  
- Relieves chronic pain and anxiety while building physical fitness through regular Yoga.  
- Improves focus, lowers stress and anxiety, and improves sleep quality through meditation.  
- Eases symptoms of other conditions such as depression and irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) through combined Ayurvedic practices and Yoga.  

Modern medicine (allopathy) and traditional Indian medicine (Ayurveda) are connected through scientific research. Today, modern laboratories analyze ancient plants to discover active ingredients for new pharmaceuticals, while genetic research helps validate Ayurveda's traditional approach to health types. Clinically, allopathy relies on fast-acting, chemically synthesized medications to treat acute infections and emergencies. In contrast, Ayurveda uses natural herbs and lifestyle adjustments to restore long-term metabolic balance.  
A Patient today can opt for Allopathic , Ayurvedic or Homeopathic treatment for a specific disease or problem.

### <u>5 &mdash; Explain the term Ayurveda?</u>

**Ayurveda is built on the idea that everything in the universe, living or non-living, is connected.** When your mind, body, and spirit live in harmony with the world around you, you enjoy good health. If this balance is broken, illness occurs. Things that can disturb this balance include genetic conditions, birth defects, physical injuries, changes in weather or seasons, aging, and your emotions. **Ayurveda (a Sanskrit term meaning "science of life" or "knowledge of life")** is one of the world's oldest holistic healing systems, developed over 5,000 years ago in India.  
Ayurveda teaches that health and wellness rely on a fine balance among the mind, body, spirit, and environment. **Its primary goal is to promote overall wellness and prevent disease, rather than simply fighting sickness after it begins. However, it also provides specific treatments for particular health conditions.**

Ayurveda is traditionally linked to Dhanvantari, known in Hindu tradition as the physician to the gods, who received this knowledge from Brahma. Its earliest principles appeared in the Atharvaveda.
According to Ayurvedic principles, every person is made of five basic universal elements: space, air, fire, water, and earth. Within the body, these elements combine to form three dynamic forces or life energies called doshas, which govern how the body functions:
- **Vata dosha:** composed of space and air
- **Pitta dosha:** composed of fire and water
- **Kapha dosha:** composed of water and earth

Like many traditional systems, Ayurveda has not undergone as much modern clinical research as conventional Western medicine. Even so, many 
Ayurvedic practices have demonstrated clear benefits for health and well-being.  

**Some observed health benefits of Ayurveda include:**
- Relieves symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis.  
- Lowers blood pressure and cholesterol levels.  
- Supports heart health and lowers the risk of heart disease through dietary guidelines.  
- Relieves chronic pain and anxiety while building physical fitness through regular Yoga.  
- Improves focus, lowers stress and anxiety, and improves sleep quality through meditation.  
- Eases symptoms of other conditions such as depression and irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) through combined Ayurvedic practices and Yoga.  

Ayurveda is divided into 8 branches Kaya Chikitsa, Bala Chikitsa, Graha Chikitsa, Shalakya Tantra, Shalya Tantra, Agada Tantra, Rasayana Tantra and Vajikarana; Each of which cover different aspects of mental, physical and sexual wellness.

### <u>6 &mdash; What do you mean by Indian Sports?</u>
Traditional Indian sports and games have been an integral part of the country's cultural heritage for centuries.  
They not only provided entertainment and recreation but also had significant health benefits and often held cultural and social significance. The following are some traditional Indian sports and games with their cultural and health-related significance:  

**Kabaddi:**  Kabaddi is a contact team sport that requires agility, strength, and teamwork. Itfosters camaraderie and teamwork among players.
 
**Gilli Danda:** This traditional game involves hitting a small wooden stick (gilli) with a larger one (danda). It requires hand-eye coordination, balance, and precision.  

**Kho-Kho:** Kho-Kho is a traditional tag sport that requires speed, agility, and teamwork. It involves two teams of twelve players each, with one team chasing and the other team defending.  

**Pachisi:** Pachisi, also known as **Chaupar**, is an ancient board game that resembles modern-day Ludo. It involves strategy, luck, and social interaction.  

**Malakhra:** It is a traditional Indian wrestling sport that originated in the state of Sindh (now in Pakistan) but is also played in parts of Gujarat and Maharashtra. It involves grappling and holds, similar to traditional wrestling (Kushti).   

**Yoga:** While not a sport in the conventional sense, Yoga is an ancient practice in India that encompasses physical postures **(asanas)**, breathing exercises **(pranayama)**, and meditation. Yoga promotes overall health and well-being, improving flexibility, strength, and mental clarity.

**Sliambam:** Silambam is a traditional martial art and sport from Tamil Nadu that involves the use of a long bamboo staff. It requires agility, coordination, and concentration. Silambam was historically practiced for self-defense and physical fitness.

**Tug of War:** Tug of War is a traditional sport played in many parts of India during festivals and cultural events. It fosters teamwork, strength, and determination among participants.

### <u>7 &mdash; What do you understand by the term Gurukul Education?</u>

The **Gurukul System** was an Ancient Indian Education system where students lived with their teacher **(guru)** in a secluded home or hermitage, devoting themselves to learning and spiritual growth. This system dates back to the Vedic Period (1500 BCE &mdash; 500BCE) and flourished until the 1200 CE.  

In the Gurukul Systems, the students resided with their gurus, Immersing themselves in a disciplined and focused learning environment. 

It featured **Holistic Learning** encompassing of **Vedic Studies, Philosophy, Spirituality, Physical Education and Vocational Skills.**   
The **Guru &mdash; Shishya** (Teacher &mdash; Student) relationships were sacred emphasizing, mutual respect, trust and devotion.

Gurukuls were usually self sufficient with students contributing to daily chores. Wealthy kings and Individuals also made monetary donations and Donations in the form of grains, spices, etc which also contributed to the running costs of the Gurukuls.

The Gurukul System didn't discriminate based on social status and learning was available to anybody who was part of the Gurukul.

The Gurukul System became obsolete during Modernization, with the rise of British India and the **Minute on Indian Education** (MEP).
