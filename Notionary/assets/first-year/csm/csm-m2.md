## Types of Case Studies

A case study is a detailed examination of a real or hypothetical situation, organization, person, event, or problem to understand it and find suitable solutions.

### By Purpose 

#### Exploratory Case Study
**Purpose:** Used to explore a new, unfamiliar, or less-understood problem. It helps identify possible issues, causes, and areas for further research.    
**Examples:**   
1. Studying why customers are shifting from traditional banking to digital banking.
2. Exploring the reasons behind low sales of a newly launched product.
Understanding why students are not actively participating in online classes.
3. Exploring challenges faced by women entrepreneurs in starting businesses.
Studying how small businesses are adopting artificial intelligence.

**Example Case:**
A bank notices that young customers are opening fewer physical branch accounts. A case study explores whether convenience, mobile banking, or customer experience is responsible.

#### Descriptive Case Study
**Purpose:** Describes a situation, organization, process, or event in detail as it exists. It mainly answers “What is happening?”  
**Examples:**
1. Describing the working process of an e-commerce company.
2. Studying the organizational structure of a bank.
3. Describing the recruitment process followed by a company.
4. Examining how a college conducts continuous internal evaluation.
5. Describing the supply-chain process of a retail company.

**Example Case:**
A case study describes how an online shopping company receives an order, processes the payment, packs the product, and delivers it to the customer.

#### Explanatory Case Study
**Purpose:** Explains why and how something happens. It focuses on relationships between causes and effects.  
**Examples:**  
1. Why did a company's sales decline after increasing prices?
2. How did employee motivation improve productivity?
3. Why did customers stop purchasing a particular brand?
4. How did digital payment systems increase transaction efficiency?
5. Why did a company experience high employee turnover?

**Example Case:** 
A company introduces flexible working hours and employee productivity increases. The case study examines how and why flexibility influenced employee performance.

#### Evaluative Case Study
**Purpose:** Evaluates the effectiveness, success, or performance of a programme, policy, strategy, or decision.
**Examples:**  
1. Evaluating the effectiveness of an employee training programme.
2. Evaluating a company's social media marketing campaign.
3. Assessing the success of a new product launch.
4. Evaluating a college's placement programme.
5. Measuring the effectiveness of a customer loyalty programme.  

**Example Case:**
A company conducts a three-month digital marketing campaign. The case study evaluates whether the campaign increased website visits, leads, and sales.

#### Diagnostic Case Study
**Purpose:** Identifies the problem, its causes, and possible solutions. It is similar to diagnosing an illness—first identify what is wrong, then determine why it happened.  
**Examples:**  

1. Finding the reasons for declining sales.
2. Identifying causes of employee absenteeism.
3. Diagnosing why a project is delayed.
4. Identifying reasons for customer complaints.
5. Finding causes of financial losses in a business.   

**Example Case:** 
A retail store experiences a 20% decline in sales. The case study investigates pricing, customer service, competition, product quality, and location to identify the main causes.

#### Problem-Solving Case Study
**Purpose:** Focuses on finding and recommending a practical solution to a specific business or organizational problem.  
**Examples:**

1. How can a company reduce employee turnover?
2. How can a bank reduce customer waiting time?
3. How can a retailer increase sales?
4. How can a start-up manage limited funds?
5. How can a company improve customer satisfaction?  

**Example Case:**
A restaurant receives many complaints about slow service. Students analyse the problem and recommend additional staff, digital ordering, and better kitchen coordination.

#### Comparative Case Study
**Purpose:** Compares two or more organizations, strategies, products, markets, or situations to identify similarities, differences, strengths, and weaknesses.  
**Examples:**

1. Comparing Amazon and Flipkart's business strategies.
2. Comparing online banking and traditional banking.
3. Comparing public-sector and private-sector banks.
4. Comparing traditional retail and e-commerce.
5. Comparing two companies' employee motivation systems.

**Example Case:**
Students compare e-commerce vs. traditional retail on price, convenience, customer experience, employment, and market reach.

#### Illustrative Case Study
**Purpose:** Provides a real-life example to explain a concept, theory, process, or business practice more clearly.  
**Examples:**

1. Using Tata Group to explain corporate social responsibility.
2. Using Amazon to explain e-commerce.
3. Using Zomato to explain platform-based business models.
4. Using a successful start-up to explain innovation.
5. Using a bank's digital transformation to explain technology adoption.  
**Example Case:**
A teacher uses a successful start-up's journey from idea → funding → product development → market launch to explain entrepreneurship and innovation.

#### Critical Case Study
**Purpose:** Examines an unusual, important, or exceptional case to understand a particular phenomenon deeply.  
**Examples:**  

1. Studying why a small start-up became a market leader.
2. Examining the failure of a major product despite strong marketing.
3. Studying a company that successfully survived a major crisis.
4. Analyzing an organization that achieved exceptionally high employee retention.
5. Studying a business that successfully entered an international market.

**Example Case:** 
A small Indian start-up becomes highly successful despite having limited financial resources. The case examines the unusual strategies responsible for its success.

### By Role Models 

Types of Case Studies in Management – By Role Models
Role-model case studies focus on successful or influential individuals whose decisions, leadership styles, strategies, values, and actions can be studied and applied by managers and students.

#### Entrepreneurial Role-Model Case Study
Focuses on how an entrepreneur identifies an opportunity, takes risks, innovates and builds a business.  
**Examples:**
- Dhirubhai Ambani – Building Reliance from a small trading business into a major conglomerate.
- Ratan Tata – Expansion and globalisation of the Tata Group.
- Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw – Building Biocon through innovation in biotechnology.
- Falguni Nayar – Creating Nykaa and identifying opportunities in beauty e-commerce.

#### Leadership Role-Model Case Study
Studies the leadership qualities, decision-making and ability to motivate employees.  
**Examples:**  
- Ratan Tata – Ethical and people-oriented leadership.
- Satya Nadella – Transforming Microsoft's organisational culture.
- Indra Nooyi – Strategic leadership and long-term business vision.
- Sundar Pichai – Leadership in a global technology organisation.

#### Innovation Role-Model Case Study

Examines how individuals use creativity, technology and innovation to solve problems.  
**Examples:**  
- Steve Jobs – Product innovation and design thinking at Apple.
- Elon Musk – Innovation in electric vehicles and space technology.
- Narayana Murthy – Building an innovation-driven IT services organisation through Infosys.
- Srikanth Bolla – Social innovation and inclusive entrepreneurship.

#### Change-Management Role-Model Case Study

Focuses on how leaders manage organisational transformation and overcome resistance to change.  
**Examples:**
- Satya Nadella – Cultural transformation at Microsoft.
- Jack Welch – Organisational restructuring at General Electric.
- Ratan Tata – Modernisation and global expansion of Tata Group.
- Reed Hastings – Netflix's transition from DVD rental to streaming.

#### Ethical Leadership Role-Model Case Study

Studies leaders who emphasise integrity, transparency, accountability and social responsibility.  
**Examples:**

- Ratan Tata – Ethical business practices and philanthropy.
- Azim Premji – Corporate responsibility and philanthropy.
- Narayana Murthy – Corporate governance and transparency.
- Anand Mahindra – Emphasis on responsible and purpose-driven business.

#### Social Entrepreneurship Role-Model Case Study
Focuses on individuals who combine business objectives with social impact.  
**Examples:**

- Muhammad Yunus – Microfinance and Grameen Bank.
- Ela Bhatt – SEWA and empowerment of women workers.
- Bunker Roy – Barefoot College and rural development.
- Harish Hande – SELCO and affordable solar energy for underserved communities.

#### Crisis-Management Role-Model Case Study

Examines how leaders respond to emergencies, failures, financial difficulties or major disruptions.  
**Examples:**
- Ratan Tata – Tata Group's response following the 26/11 Mumbai attacks.
- Howard Schultz – Starbucks' turnaround strategy during business difficulties.
- Mary Barra – Crisis management and organisational accountability at General Motors.
- Brian Chesky – Airbnb's response and business adaptation during the COVID-19 crisis.

#### Strategic-Management Role-Model Case Study

Studies how leaders develop long-term strategies, competitive advantages and growth plans.
**Examples:**

- Mukesh Ambani – Reliance Jio's digital strategy.
- Jeff Bezos – Amazon's customer-centric and long-term growth strategy.
- Warren Buffett – Value-investment and long-term business strategy.
- Indra Nooyi – PepsiCo's Performance with Purpose strategy.

#### Women-in-Management Role-Model Case Study
>
Focuses on women's leadership, career development, decision-making and overcoming organisational challenges.  
**Examples:**
- Indra Nooyi – Global corporate leadership.
- Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw – Women entrepreneurship and innovation.
- Falguni Nayar – Entrepreneurship and e-commerce.
- Arundhati Bhattacharya – Leadership and transformation in banking.

#### Employee-Motivation Role-Model Case Study

Examines how leaders create an environment that motivates employees and improves performance.  
**Examples:**

- Richard Branson – Employee empowerment at Virgin Group.
- Tony Hsieh – Organisational culture and employee experience at Zappos.
- Satya Nadella – Growth mindset and collaborative culture at Microsoft.
- Vineet Nayar – Employee-first management approach at HCL Technologies.  

#### Customer-Centric Management Role Model
Studies leaders who build competitive advantage by understanding and prioritising customer needs.  
**Examples:**  

- Jeff Bezos – Customer obsession at Amazon.
- Steve Jobs – Customer experience and product design at Apple.
- N. R. Narayana Murthy – Quality and client relationships at Infosys.
- Howard Schultz – Customer experience at Starbucks.

#### Sustainable-Management Role Model

Focuses on leaders who integrate environmental, social and economic sustainability into business decisions.  
**Examples:**  

- Anand Mahindra – Sustainability and responsible business initiatives.
- N. R. Narayana Murthy – Social responsibility through corporate initiatives.
- Yvon Chouinard – Sustainability-oriented business philosophy at Patagonia.
- Paul Polman – Sustainable business strategy at Unilever.

## Role of Researchers in Case Studies
---
Role of a Researcher in Case Studies in Management
A researcher plays a central role in a management case study by identifying a real managerial problem, collecting reliable information, analyzing the situation, and presenting findings that help managers make better decisions.

### Identifying the Management Problem
The researcher identifies the key issue or problem faced by an organization.  

**Example:** 
A company is experiencing high employee turnover. The researcher investigates whether the reasons are low salary, poor leadership, lack of promotion opportunities, or work stress.

### Defining Objectives
The researcher clearly establishes what the case study intends to examine.  

**Example:** Objectives may include:  

- To identify reasons for employee turnover.
- To study the impact of leadership style on employee satisfaction.
- To suggest measures for employee retention.

### Collecting Relevant Data

The researcher gathers primary and secondary data related to the case.  

**Examples:** Interviews with managers and employees, Employee questionnaires, Company reports, Annual reports, Sales records, HR records, Industry reports.

### Selecting Appropriate Case
The researcher decides which organization, department, event, or managerial situation should be studied.

**Example:** For studying digital transformation, the researcher may select a bank that has recently introduced mobile banking and AI-based customer services.

### Conducting Interviews and Observations
Researchers directly interact with people involved in the case and observe organisational practices.

**Example:** While studying leadership, the researcher interviews managers and observes how they communicate with their teams during a crisis.

### Maintaining Objectivity

The researcher should remain neutral and unbiased while analysing the case.

**Example:** If a researcher personally believes that remote work improves productivity, they should still report negative findings if the company's data shows reduced productivity.

### Analysing the Data
The researcher systematically analyses the collected information to identify patterns, causes, relationships, and consequences.

**Example:** Sales data may show that employee productivity declined after a new incentive scheme was introduced. The researcher investigates why the scheme failed.

### Applying Management Theories
The researcher connects the practical situation with relevant management concepts and theories.

**Example:** In an employee motivation case, the researcher may apply:
- Maslow's Need Hierarchy
- Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory
- Expectancy Theory

### Identifying Alternative Solutions
The researcher develops different possible solutions to the managerial problem.

**Example:** For declining employee motivation, alternatives could include:  
- Performance-based incentives
- Training and development
- Flexible working
- Recognition programmes
- Career advancement opportunities

### Evaluating Alternatives
The researcher compares alternatives based on cost, feasibility, risks, benefits, and organizational objectives.  

**Example:** Before recommending a new employee incentive scheme, the researcher evaluates its financial cost and likely impact on productivity.

### Making Recommendations
The researcher provides practical and evidence-based recommendations.

**Example:** After studying customer complaints, the researcher may recommend:  
- Employee customer-service training.
- A faster complaint-resolution system.
- Digital feedback mechanisms.

#### Ensuring Ethical Research
The researcher must maintain confidentiality, informed consent, honesty, and proper use of information.  

**Example:** If employees provide confidential opinions about their manager, their names should not be disclosed in the case study without permission.
