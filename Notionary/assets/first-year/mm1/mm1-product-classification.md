## <u>Definition of Product Classification</u>
---

### What is Product Classification?
**Product Classification** is the process of grouping products into specific categories based on how consumers shop for them, how long they last, or how businesses use them.   
Classifying products helps companies decide the right price, advertising strategy, and distribution methods for each type of item.


## <u>Types of Products</u>
---

Products are broadly categorized into **Consumer Goods** and **Industrial Goods** based on who uses them and how they are consumed.

### A. Consumer Goods
Consumer goods are products purchased by final consumers for personal, family, or household consumption.

## <u>Product Positioning</u>
---

### Meaning of Product Positioning
**Product Positioning** is the process of creating a distinct image and identity for a product in the mind of the customer relative to competing products.

It answers the consumer's subconscious question: *"Why should I buy this specific product instead of the alternatives?"*

*   **Example:** 
    *   **Volvo** positions its cars around **Safety**.
    *   **BMW** positions its cars around **Driving Performance**.
    *   **Tesla** positions its cars around **Clean Energy Innovation**.

### Significance of Product Positioning
1. **Creates Market Differentiation:** Helps a product stand out on crowded store shelves.
2. **Targets the Right Audience:** Clarifies who the product is built for (e.g., budget shoppers vs. luxury buyers).
3. **Guides Pricing Strategy:** A premium position justifies a higher price tag.
4. **Focuses Advertising Messages:** Ensures all marketing campaigns send one clear, consistent message.
5. **Increases Customer Loyalty:** Builds a strong emotional connection when a brand's promise aligns with customer values.


## <u>Branding</u>
---

### Meaning of Branding
**Branding** is the process of creating a unique name, symbol, logo, design, and reputation that identifies and differentiates a product or company from its competitors.

While a **product** is what you sell (e.g., a pair of running shoes), a **brand** is the personality and reputation attached to it (e.g., *Nike* symbolizing athletic achievement and motivation).

#### Key Components of a Brand
*   **Brand Name:** The spoken name (e.g., *Amul*, *Coca-Cola*).
*   **Brand Mark / Logo:** The visual icon or symbol (e.g., *McDonald's Golden Arches*).
*   **Trademark:** The legal protection given to a brand name or logo to prevent unauthorized copying.

### Significance of Branding

#### A. Significance for Consumers
1. **Easy Identification:** Helps buyers quickly pick out their favorite products in a crowded store.
2. **Assurance of Quality:** Reduces risk—customers know what level of quality to expect every time.
3. **Saves Time:** Eliminates the need to research and compare products on every shopping trip.
4. **Status & Self-Expression:** Buying certain brands helps consumers express their personality or status.

#### B. Significance for Businesses
1. **Builds Customer Loyalty:** Satisfied customers repeatedly purchase from brands they trust.
2. **Commands Premium Pricing:** People willingly pay more for a known brand than a generic unbranded alternative.
3. **Facilitates New Product Launches:** Introducing a new item under a recognized brand name ensures instant trust (e.g., a new flavor from *Cadbury* sells easily).
4. **Protects Against Competition:** A strong brand identity creates a barrier that competitors cannot easily copy.

## Takeaways

**Product Classification:** Consumer goods depend on **buying habit** (Convenience, Shopping, Specialty, Unsought), whereas Industrial goods depend on **how they enter the business process** (Materials, Capital, Supplies/Services).  

**Product Life Cycle:** Follows 4 distinct stages: Introduction &mdash; Growth &mdash; Maturity &mdash; Decline.  

**Product Positioning:** The mental space a product occupies in the customer's mind compared to competitors.  

**Branding:** Transforms a functional product into an identifiable name with an established reputation and value proposition.
